# Expert/average campaign-sim archetypes + loadout tuning tool

## What this changes and why

The campaign simulator's two purchase archetypes (`informed-saver`, `impulse-spender`)
don't actually model a competent player: side weapons are bought but never fired in
*any* simulator (`tools/simulate.ts`/`campaign-simulate.ts`/`balance-sweep.ts` never
supply a `useSideWeapon` policy to `runMission` — confirmed by reading
`src/core/replay.ts`, which defaults it to "never tap"), subscriptions are bought by
raw cheapest-price with no regard for fit, and neither archetype ever switches weapon/
shield/generator/motor *kind* mid-campaign even when a mission would reward it — despite
that being exactly what the 2026-07-10 repricing pass (kinds are "situational
sidegrades, not tiers," GAME_DESIGN.md §3/§5) was supposed to make worthwhile.

Tomáš's framing, arrived at via `/grill-me` (2026-07-11): don't model a "dumb" player —
real players will always find ways to play worse (or better) than anything we simulate,
so a deliberately bad archetype is wasted effort. Instead, model **two tiers of
genuinely smart player** that differ only in *depth of optimization*:

- **Expert** exploits the shop's own "100% sell-back, always" rule (§5) to its logical
  conclusion: before every mission, sell everything (no penalty) and rebuild the
  mathematically best affordable build for that specific mission from scratch — full
  kind-switching, no attachment to what they already own.
- **Average** is equally sensible but commits to one weapon/shield/generator/motor kind
  early and just levels it up for the whole campaign, making incremental buy/sell
  adjustments rather than tearing everything down each time — a completely normal way
  to play, not a mistake.

Target outcome: Expert clears the campaign in ~1 hour with minimal retries (matching
GAME_DESIGN §13's existing balance targets, which are and remain calibrated against a
skilled player). Average, being less deeply optimized (not worse-informed, just less
willing to fully re-optimize every shop visit), should need some real retries/replays
along the way — "if you're not optimizing everything, you might need to replay a
level" — while still always finishing (never permanently stuck, §3).

## Design decisions requiring confirmation

All resolved via this session's `/grill-me` pass — listed here for the record and so
the checklist below has something concrete to confirm against:

1. **No "dumb" archetype, ever.** Confirmed: only tiers of competent play. If the
   eventual numbers show Average still doesn't need "some grind," the fix is to make
   Expert's advantage *larger* (deeper optimization gap), never to make Average worse at
   decision-making.
2. **Expert = full sell-everything-and-rebuild per mission, driven by a precomputed
   per-mission "best build" table.** Confirmed. The table encodes, per mission: the best
   kind for each of the 7 systems (weapon/rear weapon/side weapon/shield/generator/
   motor/ship) and the best way to split a given coin budget across those systems —
   derived empirically via simulation, not hand-guessed.
3. **Average = pick one kind per core system early, never switch kind again; make
   incremental (not full-teardown) purchase adjustments.** Confirmed.
4. **Both archetypes must actually fire side weapons** — Expert on high-value targets
   only (blocker/tank/boss present), Average on a simpler "enemies on screen" trigger.
   Neither is "never fires it," which is what happens today for both.
5. **Subscription choice**: Expert picks whichever subscription best complements their
   current build; Average picks whichever is next-cheapest-and-useful (not the
   theoretically ideal fit, but not random either).
6. **The per-mission "best build" table is generated by a standalone, re-runnable
   tuning tool** (`pnpm tune`), not computed live inside the campaign sim and not a
   one-off hand-authored table. Confirmed: Tomáš will say "tune it" when he wants it
   re-run; it runs long (real wall-clock time via simulation) but costs little of the
   conversation's token budget since it's pure computation on his machine, not an agent
   loop.
7. **Guard against local-optimum lock-in**: every tuning run searches fresh (doesn't
   just refine the previous table) and reports how much better/worse the new result is
   vs. the existing table, so a stale or accidentally-bad table gets flagged instead of
   silently kept forever.
8. **Farming/replay-for-coins is explicitly deferred, not built now.** Tomáš confirmed
   "grind" can mean either more same-mission retries (already modeled via the patience
   cap) or replaying an already-cleared *easier* mission for coins (not modeled at all
   today — the campaign sim only walks forward through `MISSION_ROUTE`, never back).
   Plan: ship Expert/Average first, measure whether Average's retry counts alone
   produce a believable "needs some grind" result. Only build the farming mechanic
   (a genuinely new feature — choosing when/what to replay, not a data tweak) if
   retry-in-place isn't enough.
9. **Everything here lands in GAME_DESIGN.md** — the two-tier player model, the
   sell-everything-and-rebuild mechanic as an emergent (intended) Expert strategy, and
   the tuning tool's existence — not left to drift as tribal knowledge in this plan doc.

## The tuning algorithm (`pnpm tune`)

For each of m1-m6 (tutorials are forced-loadout, no purchase decision to tune):

1. **Kind ranking, per system, independent of budget.** Because every kind within a
   system now shares one identical price/star ladder (2026-07-10 repricing), kind choice
   and budget size are decoupled — a kind that's best at level 3 is (in the general
   case) still best at level 2 or 4, since relative kind strengths come from stats, not
   price. Run a small sim tournament per system (fix the other 6 systems at a
   reasonable mid-tier baseline, vary only the system under test across its kinds at a
   representative level) and rank kinds by mission clear-rate contribution. This is the
   same technique used earlier this session for the nova/scatter/ion comparison, just
   made systematic and repeatable instead of a one-off scratch script.
2. **Budget allocation, per mission, via marginal-value greedy search.** Starting from
   nothing, repeatedly simulate every system's "next affordable level-up" as a
   candidate and buy whichever single upgrade yields the largest marginal clear-rate
   gain per coin (small-batch sims, ~200-500 runs per candidate, not full 2000-run
   precision — this is a search step, not a final verification), until the budget is
   exhausted. This generalizes `impulse-spender`'s old fixed "always prefer core
   systems" rule into something empirically verified per mission instead of hand-
   guessed.
3. **Local-optimum guard.** After the greedy search converges, also evaluate a handful
   of structurally different alternate allocations (e.g. front-weapon-heavy vs.
   defense-heavy vs. rear/side-weapon-heavy splits) at the same budget and compare
   against the greedy result — if an alternate beats greedy by a real margin (not just
   simulation noise), report it prominently rather than silently keeping the greedy
   answer. This is the "always leave room for improvement" guard Tomáš asked for.
4. **Output**: a `tools/tune-report.md`/`.json` (same convention as
   `balance-report.md/json`) showing the winning kind + allocation per mission, plus a
   generated `RECOMMENDED_BUILD_PER_MISSION` data table in `tools/loadoutPresets.ts`
   that Expert's purchase policy consumes directly — and a delta summary against
   whatever table existed before the run.

## Complexity / blast-radius analysis

- **Kind ranking**: 7 systems × ~4-5 kinds × 6 missions × ~500 runs/candidate ≈ 90,000-
  110,000 simulated missions per full tuning run — comparable in shape to the existing
  `pnpm balance --runs 2000` sweep (96,000 simulations), just organized differently.
  Runs via `run_in_background`, not interactively watched.
- **Budget allocation greedy search**: bounded by total affordable levels across 7
  systems (≤ 5 levels × 7 systems = 35 candidate steps in the worst case) × 6 missions ×
  ~300 runs/candidate ≈ 63,000 simulated missions — same order of magnitude.
- **No change to `src/core/`** — this is entirely `tools/`-layer (simulation harness +
  new data tables), so none of it touches the constitutional "same core, real replay"
  rules, and none of it can affect a real player's save data or live gameplay.
- **campaign-simulate.ts's own test file** (`campaign-simulate.test.ts`) covers
  `applyPurchasePolicy`/`runOneCampaign` — archetype rename/redefinition needs those
  tests updated, not just left passing by accident.

## Resolution (2026-07-11)

Built exactly the simplified, post-Fable-review scope: dropped the greedy budget
allocator entirely (`INTENDED_LOADOUT_LEVELS` already supplies the level ladder); the
tuning tool (`tools/tune-loadouts.ts`, `pnpm tune`) only ranks kinds — weapon×generator
jointly at the mission's real intended level, the rest independently — and writes
`tools/recommendedKinds.generated.ts` + a spread/dominant-kind report. Also fixed the
"bought but never used" bug for both side weapons *and* supplies (`tools/policies.ts`,
shared across `simulate.ts`/`campaign-simulate.ts`/`tune-loadouts.ts`), and extracted
the `greedyPick` card-pick heuristic that was duplicated across two files while already
touching this code.

**Real measured result** (`pnpm campaign --runs 500`, replacing the earlier
informed-saver/impulse-spender pair): both `expert` and `average` complete 100% of
campaigns. `expert` measurably improves at m6 (mean retries 2.61→1.89) via tuned
kind-switching plus real side-weapon/supply usage. Neither tier shows meaningful
friction through m1-m5 (median hull ≈100% at clear for both) — confirming, with a
genuinely-competent purchase policy this time (not the earlier under-modeled
informed-saver/impulse-spender pair), that the flat-until-m6 shape is a property of the
missions/economy, not of unrealistic simulated players. Per Tomáš's own "ship it,
measure honestly, decide after" call, this is documented in GAME_DESIGN.md §13 as the
confirmed current design rather than forced into a different shape.

All CI gates green: `pnpm lint`, `pnpm build:dry`, `pnpm test` (505 passing),
`pnpm dlx fallow` (0 above threshold), `pnpm balance -- --runs 2000 --json` (0 flags,
byte-identical to before — confirming the rewrite never leaked outside the campaign
simulator), `pnpm campaign -- --runs 500` (both archetypes 100% completion).

## Fable's review (2026-07-11, pre-implementation)

Grounding: I read `tools/campaign-simulate.ts`, `src/core/replay.ts`, `src/data/items.ts`,
`src/data/missions.ts`, `tools/loadoutPresets.ts`, `src/save/SaveManager.ts`'s switch/refund
functions, GAME_DESIGN.md §1/§3/§5/§10/§13, and the nova/campaign-tension review's Resolution.
I also re-ran `pnpm campaign -- --runs 200` fresh (read-only): both current archetypes at
100% completion, retries ≈1.00 for m1–m5 (impulse-spender m4 = 1.46, m6 ≈ 2.4–2.6), median
hull 100% through m1–m5, and — load-bearing below — median coins right after clearing each
mission: m1 = 85, m2 = 105, m3 = 145–315, m4 = 535–565, m5 = 680–710, m6 = 1.1–1.8k.

### 1. The kind-ranking/budget-decoupling assumption is not sound as stated

The 2026-07-10 repricing decoupled *price* from kind. The plan slides from that to
"performance ranking is decoupled from *level*," which does not follow, and the catalog
contains direct counterexamples:

- **Motor level 1 is a four-way near-tie by construction**: rush-1, sentinel-1, and
  tactical-1 have literally identical stats (`mults` 1.0, `draws` 0.30 in `MOTOR_BASE`).
  A tournament at a "representative level" (say 3: rush 3.0× vs sentinel 0.55× — night and
  day) says nothing about which level-1 motor to buy — and level 1–2 is all Expert can
  afford through ~m4 at the real coin trajectory above.
- **Scatter's `maxTargets` grows with level** (`floor(t/2)`: 3→5) while pulse stays at 1 —
  scatter's rank vs pulse improves with level by formula, not noise.
- **Tick rounding steps at different levels per kind**: `max(2, round(base × 0.91^t))`
  gives pulse 5,5,4,4,3 and ion 7,6,6,5,5 — ±10% DPS wobble that can flip close rankings
  at specific levels.
- **Shield/generator/motor stats are hand-authored per-level tables, not shared
  exponents**: wall's cap grows ×7.3 lv1→5 while reflex's grows ×3.7; reflex's pulse
  fraction ×2.1 vs wall's ×1.75; torrent's capacity *shrinks* with level (50→35). There is
  no formula-level reason for rank stability in three of the seven systems.
- **Energy scaling makes weapon rank generator-conditional, hence level-conditional**:
  weapon `energyPerShot` grows ×1.15^t against whatever fixed "mid-tier baseline" generator
  the tournament pins. This session's own nova finding (m5: 12% on torrent vs 66% on surge)
  shows kind rank isn't even stable across *generators*; a lv5 ion/nova on a mid generator
  lives in trough-brownout where its lv1 self doesn't.

Fix, not abandonment: rank kinds **at the level the mission's budget actually buys** (or at
each affordable level), and rank the **weapon×generator pair jointly** (4×4 = 16 cells —
cheap). The other five systems can stay independent with much less risk.

### 2. The greedy search's worst enemy isn't synergy — it's objective saturation

- **Marginal clear-rate is ≈0 almost everywhere the search will look.** §13 point 5 (and my
  fresh run) shows median hull 100% and retries ≈1.00 on m1–m5 at any sane build. At
  200–500 runs/candidate, binomial noise is ±2–4pp; when every candidate's marginal gain is
  0±noise, greedy is a random-number generator with a big compute bill. The plan never says
  what the objective is once clear rate saturates. Use expected attempts (1/p) where
  p < ~0.9 and **margin metrics** (median hull, near-miss — already computed by the campaign
  sim, added yesterday for exactly this reason) as the discriminator elsewhere.
- **The local-optimum guard is too coarse for the known synergy failure.** "Weapon-heavy vs
  defense-heavy" role-splits won't surface nova+surge-vs-nova+torrent (a *pairing*, not a
  split). Joint weapon×generator ranking (point 1) removes most of this risk at the source.
- **The complexity analysis undercounts ~7×.** Each greedy step evaluates *every* system's
  next affordable upgrade (≤7–9 candidates), and there are ≤35 steps → up to ~245
  evaluations per mission, ≈440k sims at 300 runs — not 63k. Still fine in background, but
  the stated math is wrong.
- **Budget is circular and the plan's table shape can't absorb it.** Coins-at-arrival is an
  *output* of the archetype (retries, salvager bonus, star bonuses), varies per seed, and is
  tiny (85–710 coins for m1–m5). So `RECOMMENDED_BUILD_PER_MISSION` cannot be one fixed
  build per mission — it must be budget-parameterized (a per-mission *buy-priority order* is
  the natural shape, and it makes Expert's policy trivial: walk the list while affordable).
  Note the plan's own idempotence test ("same build from fresh or maxed save") is
  unsatisfiable under a fixed-build table: a maxed save liquidates to a far larger budget.
- **At these budgets, greedy is overkill anyway**: with ≤~700 coins the affordable-build
  space per mission is small enough to enumerate exhaustively (or beam-search). Simpler
  code, no local optima, no guard needed.
- **Star gates are absent from the algorithm.** It allocates coins only, but weapon lv4/lv5
  cost 16★/26★ and stars-at-arrival is play-dependent. The tuner must take a star budget and
  respect `isStarLocked`, or the table will recommend unbuyable items and Expert will
  silently degrade.
- **Tuner policy wiring is unstated and load-bearing**: candidate builds containing side
  weapons (or supplies) must be simmed *with* the side-weapon/boost policies. Simmed with
  the defaults (never tap), those slots evaluate to zero value, get no budget, and the tuner
  "proves" decision 4's whole premise wrong by construction.

### 3. Expert's sell-everything-rebuild: economically a no-op, and that's fine — implement it as a diff

The worry in the review brief (can Expert afford upgrades if it zeroes out?) is unfounded:
`switchItem`/`switchRearWeapon`/`switchShip` already implement net-cost trade-in, every kind
shares one price ladder, so a same-level kind switch costs exactly 0 and "sell all, rebuild"
nets *identical* coins to switching each slot directly. The rebuild budget is coins + full
liquidation value — always ≥ any incremental path. It's also not degenerate: §3 literally
says "the player is expected to switch kinds between missions." Recommendations:

- **Implement as per-slot diff-to-target**, not literal liquidation — same result, less
  code, no sell-ordering questions, and the 100%-refund invariant test becomes trivial.
  Keep "sell everything and rebuild" as the GAME_DESIGN narrative if Tomáš likes it.
- **Decide subscription churn explicitly**: `downgradeSubscription` refunds 100% too, so
  Expert *could* legally re-fit subs per mission. In or out — say which.
- The real risk is **redundancy, not degeneracy**: Expert ≈ informed-saver with a better
  table plus filled optional slots, and informed-saver is already at ceiling (100%
  completion, ~1.00 retries, ~30min combat vs the 50min budget). Expert will trivially pass
  its own acceptance test; the plan's entire payoff therefore rides on Average — see 4/5.

### 4. Average is under-specified exactly where it matters most

- **The kind-choice rule is load-bearing and missing.** Which kind Average commits to
  determines the entire outcome: pulse/scatter → frictionless (today's numbers); **ion →
  m5 at ~0.7% clear** at intended level → P(clear within the patience cap of 8) ≈ 5%, i.e.
  mostly *stuck* — violating the plan's own "still always finishing"; **nova → m3 at ~22%**
  → mean ~4.5 attempts, which is, ironically, the only configuration that produces the
  desired "some grind." Average's friction is a kind-lottery, not an optimization-depth
  effect. Specify: how the kind is chosen (fixed? sampled per campaign?), and report
  results **per committed kind** — an aggregate over kinds averages "stuck" with
  "frictionless" into a misleading "some retries."
- Unresolved cases the plan must answer before code: does Average commit kinds for
  rear/side/ship too, or only the core 4? What *is* an "incremental adjustment" — if it's
  "next level of the committed kind," that's exactly today's `nextCatalogUpgrade`, and
  Average is mostly a rename of impulse-spender plus side-weapon fire and sub-fit (fine,
  but say so). What's the buy order when several systems are affordable? What does
  "next-cheapest-and-useful" mean for subscriptions ("useful" is undefined)?
- **The "enemies on screen" side-weapon trigger is the dumb archetype decision 1 forbids**:
  enemies are on screen nearly the whole mission, and `SideWeaponPolicy` is evaluated every
  tick — a bare boolean trigger dumps all charges into the opening fodder wave within
  seconds. Both archetypes need edge-triggering plus a cooldown (Expert too: a blocker
  "present" for 20s would drain every railgun charge into one blocker). Give Average a
  minimally-competent trigger (e.g. ≥N enemies or any high-HP target on screen), just a
  less selective one than Expert's.

### 5. Against Tomáš's two original asks

- **Ask 1 (sims exercise the whole ship): mostly yes, with one hole the plan's own opening
  paragraph condemns.** Both archetypes *buy* supply charges (`nextSupplyUpgrade`) and no
  campaign sim ever taps one — `useBoost` is wired only in `tools/simulate.ts` behind
  `--use-supplies`; `campaign-simulate.ts` passes nothing (verified). "Bought but never
  fired" applies verbatim to supplies and the plan doesn't mention them. Add a boost policy
  (both tiers) or the whole-ship ask stays half-answered.
- **Ask 2 (expert ~1hr / average some grind): the expert half is already true today; the
  average half is very likely unreachable through optimization depth alone.**
  Impulse-spender — strictly *less* competent than the proposed Average — already reads
  100% completion, ≈1.00 retries, median hull 100% through m1–m5. And decision 1's remedy
  loop is internally contradictory: Expert is at ceiling on every metric, so "make Expert's
  advantage larger" cannot widen the gap; the gap can only widen by making missions/economy
  harder (which reopens §13's accepted flat-then-cliff decision) or by making Average worse
  (which decision 1 forbids). The plan half-knows this (test-plan item routes failure to
  decision 8), but farming addresses grind *supply*, not friction *existence*. State
  explicitly: if Average shows no friction, the next lever is mission/economy design, not
  archetype tweaking.
- "Fun the whole hour" is measured by nothing in this plan. Margin-at-clear is the closest
  existing proxy; set a target for it (e.g. Average's median hull visibly declining across
  m1–m5) or drop the phrase from the goal.

### 6. What's missing / a simpler path

- **A much cheaper design covering ~80% of the value**: keep §13's
  `INTENDED_LOADOUT_LEVELS` as the level ladder (at real budgets the level allocation is
  nearly forced anyway), add a **kind×mission clear/margin matrix** (a small tournament —
  this session used the technique twice already, and the previous Fable review explicitly
  recommended making it a standing artifact), and derive `RECOMMENDED_BUILD_PER_MISSION` =
  intended levels + matrix-best kind (weapon×generator jointly) + optional-slot fills in a
  fixed cheap order. That deletes the greedy allocator — the most fragile, most expensive,
  least-specified component — entirely. Build the allocator later only if this proves
  insufficient.
- **Dual-use the tuner as the §3 invariant checker.** A tool computing the best build per
  mission is also the "no universal best kind, no trap kind" auditor: report near-ties vs
  dominant kinds per mission. A decisive per-mission winner is a balance bug signal (nova's
  0% would have been caught at m1). This is arguably worth more than Expert itself and
  costs one extra report column.
- **A design-philosophy conflict the plan doesn't acknowledge**: §1's identity is "never
  grinding, always progressing," and §13 (2026-07-11, yesterday) explicitly *accepted*
  frictionless m1–m5 for a well-geared player as current design. "Average should need some
  real retries" quietly reopens both. Decision 9 sends everything to GAME_DESIGN.md — the
  reconciliation ("a replay or two is progress-with-texture, not grind," or a §13 revision)
  must land there too, or the two documents will disagree about whether mid-campaign
  retries are desirable.
- **Tune-report churn**: fresh-search-every-run at 200–500-run noise means back-to-back
  `pnpm tune` runs will differ and the delta summary will cry wolf every time. Give the
  delta report the same noise floor the local-optimum guard gets (flag only deltas beyond
  ~2× the binomial SE), or the guard trains its readers to ignore it.
- **Test-plan gaps**: no test that Average completes 100% of campaigns (the §3 never-stuck
  invariant — the ion/m5 case shows it is genuinely at risk, not theoretical); no test that
  table entries are coin- *and star-*feasible at campaign-realistic arrival states; no
  boost/supply-usage test; the new Expert policy must handle t1–t4 (no table entries —
  `intendedLoadoutForMission` throws for tutorials, and `MISSIONS_WITH_INTENDED_TARGET`
  exists precisely for this) the way informed-saver does; and the per-archetype seed offset
  in `campaign-simulate.ts` (`archetype === 'informed-saver' ? 0 : 10_000_000`) is a
  two-archetype hardcode the rename must generalize.

## Final decisions (post-Fable-review, 2026-07-11)

Tomáš reviewed Fable's critique and confirmed four changes to the plan above:

1. **Drop the greedy budget allocator entirely.** New shape:
   `INTENDED_LOADOUT_LEVELS` (existing) supplies the level ladder per mission (budget is
   nearly forced at real campaign coin levels anyway, so allocation search added little).
   The tuning tool now only ranks **kinds**, correctly this time: weapon×generator ranked
   *jointly* (16 cells) at the mission's own intended level — not at a fixed "representative
   level," which Fable proved is unsound (motor-1 is a literal 3-way tie; scatter/nova's
   stats scale non-uniformly with level; weapon rank is generator- and level-conditional via
   the brownout coupling this session's nova investigation already found). Shield, motor,
   rear weapon, side weapon, and ship are ranked independently at their own intended/
   representative level (lower coupling risk than weapon×generator, per Fable). Output:
   `RECOMMENDED_KIND_PER_MISSION` (renamed from `RECOMMENDED_BUILD_PER_MISSION` — no
   allocation data anymore, kind data only) in `loadoutPresets.ts`, plus a report that
   doubles as the §3 "no trap kind / no dominant kind" invariant auditor Fable recommended
   (near-tie vs. blowout margins per system per mission).
2. **Average always commits to the free starter kind** (pulse/wall/torrent/rush) for
   weapon/shield/generator/motor, for the whole campaign — never sampled, never switched.
   Deterministic and simple. Expected consequence, stated plainly rather than discovered as
   a surprise later: this is very likely the already-frictionless case in Fable's own
   analysis (today's `informed-saver`/`impulse-spender` already default to pulse and are at
   ceiling) — so Average may show little to no more "grind" than today's archetypes once
   built. That is an acceptable, honest outcome per decision 3 below, not a bug to paper
   over by picking a worse starter kind.
3. **Ship Expert/Average as scoped, measure the real gap, decide next steps after
   playing.** Confirmed: do not redesign missions to force a gap today. If Expert is
   already near-ceiling and Average-on-starter-kind shows little friction, report that
   honestly (numbers + a plain explanation of why, per Fable's finding 5) rather than
   forcing an artificial gap. GAME_DESIGN.md gets this finding recorded either way.
4. **Fix supplies (reserve boosts) in the same pass.** Same "bought but never used" bug
   class as side weapons — `campaign-simulate.ts` never wires a `useBoost` policy today.
   Both archetypes get a real supply-usage policy alongside the new side-weapon policy,
   reusing (not reimplementing) `tools/simulate.ts`'s existing `tapFirstChargedSupply`/
   `tapSuppliesReactively` logic — extract to a small shared `tools/policies.ts` rather
   than duplicating, since two real call sites now need it.

Implementation now proceeds: shared policies module (side-weapon fire, edge-triggered +
cooldown per Fable's finding 4, plus supply usage) → kind-ranking tool (`pnpm tune`) →
`expert`/`average` archetype rewrite in `campaign-simulate.ts` → test updates → full CI +
`pnpm balance`/`pnpm campaign` verification → GAME_DESIGN.md update covering the two-tier
model, the reconciliation with §1/§13's "never grinding"/"accepted flat m1-m5" language
(Fable's finding 6), and whatever the real measured Expert/Average gap turns out to be.
