# Review of `docs/plans/fable-fun-review-followup.md`

**Reviewer:** Fable (same reviewer as the 2026-07-15 fun review), 2026-07-15
**Codebase spot-checks performed:** `v2/src/core/combat.ts`, `tick.ts`, `conveyor.ts`, `timeline.ts`, `replay.ts`, `stars.ts`, `types.ts`, `v2/src/data/missions.ts`, `subscriptions.ts`, `v2/tools/campaign-simulate.ts`

## Overall verdict up front

**Not ready as written — close, but three items need a decision or a structural fix before Wave 1 starts.** The plan is honest, well-verified against the codebase (every factual claim I spot-checked was accurate, which is rare), and correctly identifies that Items 3+4+6+7 together — not any one alone — are the answer to the frictionless-midgame problem. But Item 6 has a structural convergence hole that magnitude-tuning cannot fix, Item 7 leaves two decisions unmade that will invalidate its own balance lock if decided late, and Item 3 is about to surface data that the codebase itself documents as fake. Fix those three things in the plan text and it's ready.

## Item-by-item verdicts

### Item 1 — Doc corrections: **Thumbs up**

Zero-risk, correctly split so the mission-length range waits for Item 7's real numbers. The interview corrections folded in here (rear-weapon brownout, conveyor physics) match the code exactly — `fireRearWeapon`'s comment in `combat.ts` literally says "Brownout does NOT stretch the rear weapon," and `blocksConveyor` in `timeline.ts:12` freezes the timeline only. Nothing to add.

### Item 2 — Contracts rename: **Thumbs up**

Correct diagnosis in my original review, correct minimal fix, correct Phase A/B split (string change now, truncation check later). The one thing I'd note: "Contracts" is shorter than "Subscriptions," so the Phase B overflow check is nearly free — this is the plan's least risky item and a good warm-up. Fine.

### Item 3 — Live star progress: **Concerns — surfaces data the code itself calls fake**

The Phase A claim is verified: `shieldBroke`, `spawnedCount`, `stats.kills` all live in `CoreState` (`types.ts:440-441`), star families match `stars.ts`, and a read-only derivation function is genuinely view-layer-only. The design call (two binary icons + one countdown, not four time tiers) is the right restraint.

But there's a hole the plan walked past. `missions.ts` (m1's `stars` entry, ~line 330) documents that **m1–m5's time-star tiers are a synthetic ±1-2s spread around a single zero-variance value** — "not four meaningfully different skill bars, just a tie-break." My original review credited the docs for admitting the time-stars were fake; this plan now proposes putting a **live countdown to those fake thresholds in the combat HUD**. A countdown where T1 through T4 whoosh past within a 4-second window will read as broken, and worse, it will show the player a number they cannot meaningfully influence (the tiers collapsed precisely because greedy play produces near-identical durations). Surfacing the tie-break makes its tie-break-ness visible.

**Required change:** Item 3's Phase A should include re-anchoring m1–m5 time-star tiers to humanly-distinguishable spreads (or the countdown should be scoped to m6, whose tiers the same comment says are real). Doing the HUD without this ships a tension meter pointed at noise — the exact opposite of what this item exists to create. Note this also interacts with Item 6 (below) and with Item 7 (a new mission's tiers should be anchored with real spread from day one).

Minor: decide whether "at risk" indicators show for stars the player already earned in previous runs — an "all-kills at risk!" warning for a star you permanently own is noise.

### Item 4 — Tap-to-target: **Thumbs up with two required additions**

First, the physics premise holds: I verified damage is applied instantly to whatever `selectTargets` returns — there is no projectile occlusion in core, so the front weapon *can* hit a rear-queue booster. And front-weapon-only is more meaningful than my prompt-question feared: `fireRearWeapon` fires a **centered slice around the mid-queue index**, so the rear weapon does *not* auto-clear the back of the queue — the rearmost enemy (where the booster lives) is reachable only via front-weapon priority. The scoping decision is sound.

On "free and instant trivializing the decision": it half-does, and the plan's own verification criterion proves it. The real cost of targeting is positional — while you snipe the booster, the front-most enemies keep closing and will collide (`conveyor.ts:21-25`) — so there *are* situations where targeting deep hurts you. That's a genuine trade. But the plan's Phase A success criterion is "confirm prioritizing them measurably helps... if it doesn't move the numbers, something's wrong." **That's the wrong test, or at least half of it.** A zero-cost mechanic that strictly helps is a chore (always keep the mark on the scariest thing), not a decision. The interesting verification is the converse: build a *naive* policy ("always mark the biggest rear enemy, always") and confirm it sometimes **loses** to no-targeting — e.g. on swarm-heavy waves where ignoring the front gets you collided to death. If naive targeting never loses, the mechanic is a reflex tax and needs a cost (even a soft one, like the mark clearing on shield break). Add that second policy comparison to Phase A verification.

Two specification gaps:

1. **Multi-target interaction is unspecified.** `fireShipWeapon` applies `falloff` by target index (`Math.pow(stats.weaponFalloff, index)`). When `effectiveTargets > 1`, is the priority target index 0 (full damage even at the back of the queue, rest of the shots fill from front-most)? Almost certainly yes, but it changes balance math and the plan doesn't say. Specify before writing the mutator.
2. **No onboarding.** The plan adds the game's fourth-ever verb and its signature payoff (the booster) — and no item teaches it. t1–t4 each exist to teach one mechanic; tap-to-target gets a pointer handler and a reticle. At minimum, m3b needs a narrator line ("Mark the booster — your forward battery will do the rest") or the booster lesson lands on players who don't know the verb exists. Add to Item 4 or Item 7's Phase A scope.

Otherwise this is the plan's best-specified item — the determinism checklist (hash inclusion, replay taps mirroring `boostTaps`, `TargetPolicy` mirroring existing policy hooks) matches the actual code structures exactly.

### Item 5 — Chain enabler economics: **Thumbs up**

Verified in `subscriptions.ts`: `oc-core` and `pierce-lance` are in sub-offensive Lv2 (`price: 600` on top of Lv1's 350 = the 950 the plan cites) and `res-sync` in sub-defensive Lv2. The interview correction — that chain scarcity is economic, not call-count — also checks out: m1 alone has 7 `supportCallTicks`, so my original "1–3 calls" assumption was wrong and the plan is right to fix the price gate instead of call frequency. Pure data change, correct verification list, and the `SaveManager` owned-cards interaction is already flagged in File hygiene. One optional note: after losing two of its five-ish cards to Lv1, sub-offensive Lv2 still costs 600 — worth a glance at whether Lv2's thinned pool still justifies its price, but that's a tuning nicety, not a blocker.

### Item 6 — Opt-in blocker elites: **Thumbs down as specified — structural hole plus missing determinism spec**

This is the item where the fix doesn't match the diagnosis. My original suggestion was a *risk lever*: hold the blocker for a bigger reward **at real risk**. The plan's answer is "hold longer → bigger bonus; cost = blocker chip damage + elapsed time," to be tuned via sim until "holding is a real, felt trade-off." I don't believe the tuning can get there, because the cost side is structurally hollow:

- `blocksConveyor` **freezes new spawns** (`timeline.ts:12`) — so while you hold, no new pressure arrives.
- Already-spawned enemies still advance and can be killed. So the optimal line is: **clear the live queue first, then hold the lone blocker in complete safety** while its chip damage (m2's blocker: `shotDamage` ~small, one shot per 0.8–3s) is out-regenerated by shield pulses. Once the queue is empty, holding is risk-free by construction.
- The only remaining cost is time — and per Item 3's finding, m1–m5's time-stars are 1–2s-wide synthetic tie-breaks, so *any* hold forfeits all four, collapsing the decision to "never hold if chasing time stars, always hold otherwise." That's a settings toggle, not a mid-run gamble.

So simulation/optimal play converges on **always-hold (after clearing), with a boring safe wait** — the exact fake-decision failure mode the review question anticipated, plus a new one: optimal play now deliberately creates idle stretches, which `pnpm pacing`'s own IDLE_STRETCH detector exists to forbid. Tuning magnitudes (bonus size, chip damage) can't fix a shape problem.

**Required change — pick a structure where the risk can't be drained before the hold:** e.g. (a) the elite charge only accrues while *other enemies are alive on screen* (hold-under-pressure is the whole decision — clearing the queue stalls the charge), or (b) the held blocker escalates (fire rate or damage ramps the longer it's held), or (c) the bonus decays after a peak window so overholding wastes it. Any of these makes "when do I stop holding" a live judgment; the current spec makes it arithmetic. The plan's sim-policy methodology (hold vs. burn vs. judgment policies) is exactly right — it just needs to be pointed at a mechanic whose trade-off can structurally exist.

Also: unlike Item 4's careful checklist, Item 6 specifies **no determinism plumbing** — a hold-charge timer is new `CoreState`, needs hash inclusion, and if "holding" is expressed through any new player input, it needs replay recording. And the plan never says *what the holding verb actually is*. With auto-fire on, the front weapon burns the front-most enemy; the only ways to not kill a blocker are to toggle the weapon off (a lovely accidental synergy with the energy-management identity — arguably the best version of this mechanic!) or tap-target past it (the Item 4 synergy the plan mentions) — but when the blocker is the *last enemy alive*, both front and rear weapons converge on it and holding requires disabling weapons entirely. Whether that's the intended expression or a problem, the plan must say.

### Item 7 — New mission + booster: **Concerns — right idea, two decisions must be made now, not in Phase B**

**On the "does one hard mission fix flatness or just move the cliff" question:** it doesn't fully fix the curve, and the plan shouldn't claim more than it delivers — but it's still worth building. The shape goes from flat→cliff to flat→**spike**→flat→cliff. The genuine wins: the skill loop gets tested while there's still campaign left to apply the lesson to (m6's test currently arrives at the end, when learning is pointless), and the toggle/targeting verbs get a mandatory rehearsal. The genuine non-win: m4 and especially m5 (measured 155s, the shortest, easiest mission in the game) will read *flatter than before* by contrast — a player who survives a near-m6-tier m3b then coasts through two easy missions is an ANTICLIMAX pattern the pacing tool measures per-mission and will therefore never flag, because no tool measures the cross-mission curve. **Add an acceptance criterion at campaign level:** the retry/margin distribution across the route should show m3b as a clear local peak *and* m6 still the global peak, and m4/m5 floors should be revisited (even slightly) after m3b lands. Otherwise this recreates my original complaint with one bump in it. Also watch the "never grinding" invariant from the other side: "closer to m6's tier" difficulty against a mid-campaign wallet could push the average archetype's retries at m3b well above ~1 — `pnpm campaign`'s 100%/100% check catches *stuck*, not *grindy*. Set a retry ceiling for the average archetype at m3b, not just a clear-rate floor.

**Unmade decision #1 — is m3b skippable?** The plan says insert `['m3','m3b'], ['m3b','m4']` into `MISSION_UNLOCK_EDGES` but never says whether `['m3','m4']` is *removed*. I checked `missions.ts:630` and `SaveManager.ts:231` — unlock is purely "any incoming edge satisfied," so if the old edge stays, m4 unlocks straight from m3 and the "second real test" is **optional content** that the players who most need testing will route around. If the old edge goes, m3b is a mandatory wall (softened by the campaign-sim guarantee). These are two different games; `MISSION_ROUTE` in `campaign-simulate.ts` only models one of them. Decide explicitly and write it down.

**Unmade decision #2 — booster adjacency rule, and it can't wait for Phase B.** The plan says the booster buffs "the one enemy directly ahead of it *in spawn order*." Enemies have independent speeds and never interact spatially — a fast enemy spawned after the booster will visually *overtake* it, at which point the spawn-order neighbor is visually behind the booster and the on-screen buff-link (Phase B's stated readability requirement) points backwards. If Phase B discovers this, the fix is changing the core rule to nearest-ahead-*by-distance* — which changes combat outcomes and **invalidates the already-locked m3b balance**. This is the single strongest case in the plan where deferring the screenshot risks redoing Phase A work. Decide the rule now; I'd argue for distance-based (it's what the player will see) with the mission's wave composition keeping speeds sane either way. The planned no-stacking tests are good; add a test for the overtake case and for booster-buffs-booster adjacency.

**On regen-buff-only vs. t3's guardian:** defensible, with one dependency. Mechanically it reuses `regenPerTick`, but the *decision* differs: t3's self-regen guardian teaches "you need more damage than its regen" (a threshold problem, solved by card picks); the booster teaches "kill order matters, and you now have a verb to express it" (a targeting problem, solved by Item 4). That's genuinely a different lesson — **but only if tap-to-target ships and the buff link is visible.** Without Item 4, the booster is exactly the review's original complaint ("must be prioritized" with no way to prioritize); without the visual link, it's the same lesson as t3 felt from the outside. The plan's own Phase B note admits this ("or the mechanic is invisible even though it works"). So: fine as scoped, and the deferred damage-buff variant is the right call — but this hard-couples Item 7 to Item 4, which the sequencing correctly respects.

## The two-wave structure

**Sound overall, with three specific leak points.** The core argument holds: this codebase's sim/balance/campaign/pacing toolchain genuinely can exercise every core mechanic headlessly (I verified the policy-hook and replay-tap patterns Item 4 mirrors are real), and "balance is the substantive work, visuals verify it" is the right posture *for this game*, whose combat is a deterministic core with a rendering skin. The rule that Phase B code shouldn't be written blind (rather than written-and-unverified) is the right discipline.

Where deferring the screenshot carries real redo risk:

1. **Booster adjacency (above)** — the worst case; a Phase B readability discovery forces a core-rule change after balance lock. Fix: decide distance-based adjacency now.
2. **Tap ergonomics vs. sim fidelity.** Item 4's balance verdict comes from a frame-perfect policy that re-marks targets instantly. A human on a phone tapping small, fast, overlapping sprites will realize a fraction of that benefit and will mis-tap. The core API survives Phase B intact, but the *tuning conclusions* ("prioritizing the booster makes m3b clearable at X%") may be calibrated to superhuman input — and m3b's difficulty floor is being set against that calibration. Mitigation: give the sim policy a human-ish reaction delay (the toggle policies from the simulator-fidelity work presumably faced the same issue), and expect a Phase B re-tune pass on m3b, not just a look.
3. **Item 3's design call made blind** ("two icons + countdown") is explicitly provisional in the plan — fine — but per my Item 3 note, the *data* under the countdown is the real problem, and that's a Phase A fix.

## Sequencing

The order is right, and notably the two most important orderings are already correct: Item 4 (targeting) before Item 7 (the mission whose signature enemy requires targeting), and Item 7 last so the toolchain is warm. Two adjustments:

- **Move the time-star re-anchoring (my Item 3 addition) before Item 6's balance work**, because Item 6's hold-cost analysis depends on whether time-stars are real costs or 1-second tie-breaks. As sequenced, Item 6 would be tuned against fake time pressure.
- **Item 6 should be re-specified (the structural fix) before its slot, or swapped after Item 7.** As written it's the plan's only item that needs a design decision mid-implementation; doing m3b first and Item 6 afterward — with knowledge of whether m3b uses blockers — costs nothing and lets the elite mechanic be tuned against the full final mission set. Also, `pnpm campaign` runs during Item 6 verification would otherwise be repeated after Item 7 anyway.

## What's missing

Beyond the two acknowledged deferrals (endless mode, ion dominance — both reasonable to track separately):

1. **The fake time-star tiers** (detailed under Item 3) — the plan surfaces them without fixing them. This is the biggest genuine gap.
2. **The economy critique is silently dropped.** My review's "the shop is a fill bar with one decision" point gets no decision in the table, no plan item, and no line in "Explicitly out of scope." Deliberately not acting on it is a defensible call (100% sell-back was also praised, and the switch-cost economy work in recent commits may have changed the picture) — but an explicitly-confirmed-decisions plan should confirm *that* decision too. Add it to out-of-scope with a sentence of rationale.
3. **No onboarding for the new verb** (detailed under Item 4) — a fourth verb with zero teaching in a game whose tutorials are its most disciplined feature.
4. **No cross-mission difficulty-curve criterion** (detailed under Item 7) — every verification tool in the list is per-mission or pass/fail; nothing asserts the campaign *shape* that this whole plan exists to fix. One added check on the campaign sim's per-mission retry/margin table closes this.
5. Minor: m5's measured 155s makes it both the shortest and (per the original telemetry) an easy mission sitting directly before the finale — the plan records the measurement in Item 1 and does nothing with it. At least note it as accepted.

## Summary of required changes before implementation

1. **Item 6:** replace magnitude-tuning with a structural anti-idle mechanism (charge-under-pressure, escalation, or decay); specify the holding verb and the determinism plumbing. *(Blocker.)*
2. **Item 7:** decide now — old `['m3','m4']` edge removed or kept (mandatory vs. skippable m3b), and booster adjacency by distance vs. spawn order. Add a retry ceiling and a campaign-shape acceptance criterion. *(Blocker for Item 7's start, not for Wave 1's start.)*
3. **Item 3:** re-anchor m1–m5 time-star tiers (or scope the countdown to m6) before surfacing them. *(Blocker for Item 3's Phase B, should land in Phase A.)*
4. **Item 4:** add the "naive targeting sometimes loses" policy comparison; specify multi-target/falloff interaction; add an onboarding beat. *(Small, do during Item 4.)*
5. Add the economy-critique non-decision to "Explicitly out of scope."

Everything else — Items 1, 2, 5 wholesale, the Phase A/B doctrine, the wave ordering, the file-hygiene section — is ready as written. The plan's factual grounding is excellent; its weakness is the two places (Items 6 and 7) where a "confirmed decision" is actually a confirmed *direction* with the decision still inside it.
